## The one thing you should read first

**A headline claim was wrong and is now corrected.** The paper said transformers
add nothing measurable on DAIGT V2. That comparison let the classical models read
the whole essay while the transformers read 128 tokens, which is 33.5% of a mean
DAIGT V2 essay. Held to the same window the best classical model goes from 0.90%
to 2.10% error against BERT's 0.84%, p<1e-6. Both tokenizer windows agree.

The paper now says the defensible thing instead: a bag-of-words model reading a
whole essay matches a transformer reading its opening. That is a claim about
information access rather than architecture, and it is a better result than the
one it replaces, because it also puts a question mark over published
classical-beats-transformer results that never matched text budgets.

## Defects found and fixed

1. **Split misstated.** Methods said 80/10/10. The code does 72/8/20. Row counts
   confirm. Fixed in the paper and in both split scripts, where the error started.
2. **Content arm misdescribed.** Methods claimed stopword removal and lemmatisation;
   the decomposition does neither. That step lives in `classical_full.py`. The arm is
   an unfiltered bag-of-words, which makes the HC3 parity finding stronger, not weaker.
3. **Arms shared a length channel.** Both could read document length. Closed on both
   sides. Both findings survived and strengthened.
4. **Lead claim was untested.** "Indistinguishable" had no CI and no test. Now carries
   McNemar and paired bootstrap, plus five-partition replication.
5. **Noise floor came from the wrong cell.** The 0.0036 seed range is HC3/BERT and was
   being applied to a DAIGT V2 DeBERTa comparison. Replaced by paired tests throughout.
6. **Group-aware splits were undersold.** Leakage is zero by construction. The paper
   had instead reported a 0.67% small-scale number describing none of its own runs.
   Measured properly: 0 of 10,732 group-aware, 570 of 10,762 (5.30%) naive.
7. **A wrong number was retired.** The "11.2-11.3% naive leakage" figure in
   `build_full_splits.py` was never measured and is wrong. True value 5.30%.

**One thing I flagged that turned out to be fine:** the bibliography. Every DOI, URL
and arXiv id resolves with a matching title. The 9 uncited keys never reach the
rendered bibliography. I left it alone.

## New results now in the paper

- **Truncation-matched comparison.** Above. Transformers lead on both corpora at one
  text budget.
- **Length control.** DAIGT V2 content advantage widens 7.9x to 10.6x. HC3 parity
  becomes a 2.91-point advantage for orthography. An earlier version of this control
  collapsed for regularisation-scale reasons, not length. Do not quote the un-rescaled row.
- **Five-partition replication.** DAIGT V2 content leads on all five, p<1e-6. HC3
  surface-content is significant on none of five and **changes sign between them**,
  which is the strong form of the null.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Datasets}

DAIGT V2~\cite{Thedrcat2023daigtv2} contains 44,868 argumentative student essays, 27,371 human-written from the PERSUADE corpus and 17,497 machine-generated by a mixture of 2023-era systems including GPT-3.5, Llama-2, Mistral-7B and Falcon-180B, and was assembled for a public detection competition~\cite{Kaggle2023llmdetect}. The human side derives from the PERSUADE corpus of student argumentative writing~\cite{Crossley2023persuade}. HC3~\cite{Guo2023close} contains question-answer pairs from five English sources, contrasting human answers with a single generator, GPT-3.5-Turbo-0301, over 85,449 rows~\cite{HelloSimpleAI2023hc3}. Both were class-balanced for training, giving 34,994 DAIGT V2 rows and 53,806 HC3 rows, and partitioned once into fixed 72/8/20 train, validation and test splits used unchanged by every model reported here, 25,196/2,800/6,998 rows for DAIGT V2 and 38,785/4,289/10,732 for HC3. The partition is duplicate-group aware, so no two documents with identical normalised content are placed on opposite sides of a boundary. Training partitions are balanced to within 0.002 of parity, which matters for interpreting the degenerate-prediction control in Section~\ref{sec:confound}.

A duplicate-detection pass over the unbalanced corpora finds DAIGT V2 essentially free of internal duplication, six rows in six groups, 0.01\%, with no text under both labels. HC3 carries 6,118 duplicate rows in 5,986 groups, 7.16\%, plus 476 rows labelled machine-generated that are collection artefacts, typically stored API failure messages. That duplication is why the split is group-aware, and the choice is worth more than it sounds. No HC3 test document recurs in training or validation under the group-aware partition, whereas under a plain stratified split of the same balanced sample 570 of 10,762 do, 5.30\%. DAIGT V2 leaks nothing either way. No number in this paper is inflated by memorised duplicates.

\subsection{Models and the combinations evaluated}

Five model families are evaluated. Three are classical, Naive Bayes, logistic regression and a linear support vector machine, each run under bag-of-words and TF-IDF for six classical configurations per dataset. Earlier work at full scale reported only the winning representation per model, so we report the complete grid. Two are transformers, \texttt{bert-base-uncased} and \texttt{microsoft/deberta-v3-base}, fine-tuned over a grid of learning rate $\{2\mathrm{e}{-5}, 3\mathrm{e}{-5}\}$, batch size $\{16, 32\}$ and weight decay $\{0.01, 0.1\}$, sixteen configurations per dataset, with the operating point selected on validation F1.

Classical models receive text through the normalisation pipeline described below. Transformers receive raw text truncated to 128 tokens, a constraint inherited from the study this corpus was built for and quantified in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\subsection{The surface-content decomposition}

The central instrument of this paper separates how much of a benchmark's human-versus-machine separability is available from surface form alone and how much from content alone. Three arms are compared on identical splits.

The \emph{surface-only} arm uses 47 orthographic features and never reads word identity. These cover punctuation character rates, whitespace behaviour including the count of spaces preceding punctuation, casing statistics, document and sentence length, non-ASCII and emoji rates, and digit rates. Because no lexical item enters the feature vector, this arm cannot access content by construction.

The \emph{content-only} arm applies the same surface normalisation the classical pipeline begins with, lowercasing and replacing every character outside \texttt{[a-z\textbackslash s]} with a space, then vectorises to bag-of-words. It applies no stopword removal and no lemmatisation, so it is an unfiltered content model and not a reduced one. Punctuation, casing and non-ASCII characters cannot survive the transformation.

One channel survives into both arms. The surface arm reads character, word and sentence counts directly, and raw bag-of-words rows sum to document length. Length is a documented confound on both corpora, so we close it rather than assume it away, with a 42-feature surface arm stripped of every document-size feature and a content arm whose rows are normalised to unit total and rescaled so regularisation bites equally hard in both. Section~\ref{sec:decomp} reports what changes.

The \emph{full} arm is the fine-tuned transformer on raw text. Surface-only and content-only share one classifier family, logistic regression, so they are directly comparable to each other. The transformer differs in family and is reported as a reference point rather than as a matched arm, and we draw no conclusion from a transformer-to-classical difference alone.

\subsection{Statistics and confound checks}
\label{sec:confound}

Every reported figure comes from a committed script writing JSON, and every transformer baseline is read from the deployed checkpoint's own record, never from the maximum over its hyperparameter grid. An earlier version of this analysis compared a grid maximum against a single fixed configuration and reported differences that did not exist, which is why baselines are now checkpoint-matched throughout.

We report error rate alongside F1 because at this accuracy ceiling F1 differences compress, and macro alongside weighted F1 so that no result can be produced by class skew. Seed spread over three seeds is reported as a range and only ever for the cell it was measured on, never borrowed across datasets or model families, since fine-tuning outcomes vary materially with initialisation and data order~\cite{Dodge2020fine,Mosbach2020stability}. It is an order-of-magnitude indication, not a test statistic, so no comparison in this paper rests on it. Where two models are compared on the same test set we use paired evidence instead, exact-binomial McNemar on the discordant predictions together with a paired bootstrap 95\% interval on the error difference over 10,000 resamples. Because a bootstrap resamples one test set and says nothing about how a corpus was partitioned, the decomposition is also repeated over five group-aware partitions, varying only the split seed. We do not run parametric tests at $n \le 30$.

Three controls guard the specific claims made below. First, a \emph{pipeline-fires} control. Our whitespace cleaning produces bit-identical predictions for BERT on HC3, which alone would be indistinguishable from cleaning that silently failed to run. The same pipeline applied to DAIGT V2 does change BERT's predictions, since emoji removal is visible to its tokeniser, establishing that the pipeline fires and that the null is specific to whitespace. Second, a \emph{tokenisation} control, comparing token identifiers for minimally different strings to establish what each tokeniser can represent. Third, a \emph{label-free} control, scoring 400 inputs per condition that carry no human-versus-machine information at all, including uniform random character strings, token-shuffled text, punctuation-only strings and the empty string. Without this last control a confident prediction on corrupted input is indistinguishable from a learned response to it.

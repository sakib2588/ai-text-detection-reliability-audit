\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

Every number in this paper is produced by a committed script that writes a JSON file, and no number was transcribed from a session log. The mapping from claim to artefact is as follows, and the naming below is the naming in the released repository.

The corpus statistics and leakage figures in Table~\ref{tab:corpora} come from the two full-corpus audits and from the split verifier, which measures leakage directly on both split variants rather than estimating it. The eight-configuration comparison in Table~\ref{tab:allmodels} and the ROC curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:roczoom} and Fig.~\ref{fig:roc} come from a single evaluation script that reads transformer scores from each deployed checkpoint's own record and refits the classical grid on the same partition. The decomposition in Table~\ref{tab:decomp} and Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}, together with the length-controlled arms, comes from one script that also writes per-document predictions, which is what makes the paired tests in Section~\ref{sec:decomp} possible rather than asserted. The five-partition repeat in Table~\ref{tab:multisplit} varies only the split seed and reproduces the seed-42 figures exactly, which is its self-check. The matched-budget comparison in Table~\ref{tab:budget} uses offset mapping to recover the exact character span each tokeniser kept, with no decode round-trip. The attributions in Fig.~\ref{fig:shap} and Table~\ref{tab:shap} use an exact linear explainer over the same surface featuriser the decomposition uses, imported rather than reimplemented so the two cannot drift apart. The label-free control in Table~\ref{tab:labelfree} and the perturbation results in Table~\ref{tab:adv} come from two further scripts, and the ensemble in Table~\ref{tab:ensemble} is computed from the deployed checkpoints' saved probability arrays with the mixing weight selected on validation only.

The partition is built once, by a script that asserts no duplicate-content group crosses a boundary, and is reused unchanged by every model and every training seed. Transformer training uses a fixed seed for initialisation and data order, with best-epoch selection on validation weighted F1 and early stopping at patience two. We record that GPU training in this environment is not bitwise deterministic, so a rerun of the same seed can select a different best epoch, and this is why no claim in the paper rests on a seed range.

Three practices are worth naming because each was adopted after an error that reached a draft. Transformer baselines are read from the deployed checkpoint's own record rather than from the maximum over its hyperparameter grid, after an ablation that compared a grid maximum against a fixed configuration reported differences that did not exist. Seed spread is quoted only for the cell it was measured on, after a spread measured on one corpus and model family was applied as a noise floor to a comparison on another. And a claimed leakage rate that had no located source file was removed and then measured, at which point it turned out to be 5.30\% under a naive split and zero under the split this paper uses.

A single notebook reproduces the preprocessing, both transformers at their deployed configurations and the ensemble, reading each configuration from the checkpoint record at runtime rather than from a value typed into the notebook.

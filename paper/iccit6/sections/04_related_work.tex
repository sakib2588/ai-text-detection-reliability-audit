\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Detection on these benchmarks.} HC3 was introduced together with a RoBERTa detector at 99.82 F1~\cite{Guo2023close}. That established both the corpus and the accuracy regime the field has worked in since. The regime narrows to roughly 91.7\% under semantic-invariant tasks~\cite{Su2023plus}. Competitive DAIGT V2 accuracy has been reached with character n-grams and no transformer~\cite{Zhou2024exploiting}. That result is consistent with ours, but it was reported without the surface-content separation that would explain it. DeBERTa has been paired with stylometric features~\cite{Yadagiri2025aigenerated}, and style embeddings alone have been used to detect~\cite{Socolof2024style}. Both combine the two channels to raise a score, where we hold them apart to measure one.

\textbf{The closest prior result.} The HC3 whitespace convention was identified in~\cite{Tian2023multiscale}, which released a cleaning kit. Its appendix reports that a detector consisting of a single test for one token identifier reaches 82.12 F1 on HC3 at sentence level, above the 81.89 it quotes for a fine-tuned RoBERTa. Our document-level equivalent of that rule reaches 94.22 weighted F1. The difference is sentence against document, not a disagreement. That work is the closest existing statement of this paper's HC3 finding.

\textbf{Shared benchmarks and shortcut learning.} RAID~\cite{Dugan2024raid}, M4~\cite{Wang2024m4} and SemEval-2024 Task 8~\cite{Wang2024semeval} make detectors comparable across generators, domains and attacks. They do not ask what any one condition is separable by. That is the axis added here, and the reason this measurement would apply to them unchanged. The pattern is not specific to text detection. Vision datasets are identifiable from their own images~\cite{Torralba2011unbiased}. In natural language inference, a model reading only the hypothesis reaches far above chance~\cite{Gururangan2018annotation,Poliak2018hypothesis}, and~\cite{Geirhos2020shortcut} gives the general account. Our surface arm is a partial-input baseline transferred to this task, except that it removes a property of the text rather than a span of it. The caveat that governs how such a baseline may be read comes from~\cite{Feng2019misleading}. A partial-input model scoring highly shows a dataset is cheatable. One scoring poorly does not show it is clean, since the restricted representation may fail to express an artefact that is present. We therefore report the surface arm as an upper bound on surface-carried separability, not as a measurement of it.

\textbf{Robustness and fairness.} HC3-trained detectors attacked with misspellings and homoglyphs fall from 99.88 F1 to 33.57\% accuracy~\cite{Antoun2023towards}. Deployed detectors misclassify non-native English writing as machine-generated at high rates~\cite{Liang2023gpt}. That bears on our result, since a detector whose separability rests on orthographic regularity should concentrate its errors on writers whose habits differ from the corpus it was fitted to.

\textbf{The gap.} None of the above measures how much of a benchmark's separability is carried by surface form. Unlike~\cite{Tian2023multiscale}, which removes one cue, and~\cite{Dugan2024raid,Wang2024m4}, which compare detectors across conditions, this paper measures each corpus with two matched arms, locates the result per sub-corpus, and checks whether the detector can read the cue at all.
